# Private Limited Company Compliance Checklist 2026: What to Do After Registration

Your Certificate of Incorporation starts the compliance clock. A new private limited company must usually hold its first board meeting and appoint its first auditor within 30 days, issue share certificates within 2 months, and file INC-20A within 180 days. After that, it has board meetings, an AGM, annual MCA filings and an income tax return to manage every year.

This private limited company compliance checklist is for founders who have just registered, or who registered earlier and are unsure what they may have missed. It covers the Companies Act deadlines first, then the monthly tax work that usually runs alongside them.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
- First 30 days: hold the first board meeting and appoint the first auditor.
- First 2 months: issue share certificates to the subscribers.
- First 180 days: receive the subscription money and file INC-20A, where it applies.
- Every year: hold board meetings and an AGM, then file AOC-4, MGT-7 or MGT-7A, and the income tax return.
- For FY 2025-26: MCA allows AOC-4 without additional fees until 31 December 2026, and MGT-7 or MGT-7A until 31 January 2027. AGM deadlines have not moved.

## What Must a New Private Limited Company Do in Its First Year?

Start with the dates below and count them from the date on your Certificate of Incorporation. Share the list with whoever handles your accounts and filings, so one person is clearly responsible for each item.

| Deadline | What to do | Why it matters |
| Within 30 days | Hold the first board meeting | Required under section 173 of the Companies Act, 2013 |
| Within 30 days | Board appoints the first auditor | Required under section 139(6). If the board misses this, members must appoint the auditor within 90 days of incorporation |
| Within 15 days of appointment | File ADT-1 for the first auditor | The legal position is debated, but filing is the practical choice (see below) |
| Within 2 months | Issue share certificates to subscribers | Required under section 56(4) |
| Within 180 days | Subscribers pay for their shares, and the company files INC-20A | Required under section 10A for companies with share capital, where it applies |
| Before business grows | Set up accounts, invoices, statutory registers and a compliance calendar | Late records make every later filing harder |

### Why INC-20A deserves your attention

INC-20A is the declaration that subscribers have paid for the shares they agreed to take. A missed filing can lead to a penalty of ₹50,000 on the company and ₹1,000 per day on each officer in default, up to ₹1,00,000 per officer. The Registrar can also start action to remove the company's name from the register.

Open the company bank account early, so that the subscription money has a clear payment trail before you file.

### Do you need ADT-1 for the first auditor?

This is one of the few items where practitioners disagree. The revised ADT-1, in use since 14 July 2025, lets a company report a first-auditor appointment. But the underlying filing rule was not changed, and the Institute of Company Secretaries of India asked MCA for clarity in April 2026. As of June 2026, reports said MCA had not replied.

Our practical view is simple: file it within 15 days of the board meeting. The AOC-4 form checks for the auditor's ADT-1 details, so leaving it out can cause trouble at your first annual filing.


## What Should Happen at the First Board Meeting?

Section 173(3) requires at least 7 days' notice for a board meeting, unless the rules for shorter notice are followed. Use the first meeting to settle the items that later filings depend on.

CHECKLIST: FIRST BOARD MEETING
[ ] Note the Certificate of Incorporation, MOA and AOA
[ ] Appoint the first auditor and record the appointment
[ ] Take each director's disclosure of interests (MBP-1)
[ ] Take each director's declaration that they are not disqualified (DIR-8)
[ ] Approve opening the company bank account and name who can operate it
[ ] Approve the issue of share certificates
[ ] Decide who keeps the statutory registers and minutes
[ ] Agree the financial year and the accounting setup
[ ] Review which registrations apply, such as GST, professional tax or a shop licence

Also put the company's name, registered office address and CIN on your letterheads, invoices and official notices, as section 12 requires. Founders often miss this while rushing to design their branding.


## What Filings Does a Private Limited Company Have Every Year?

Every company must keep its accounts, have them audited, and report to both MCA and the Income Tax Department. This applies even if the company had little or no business during the year.

| Compliance | When it is due | Who it applies to |
| Board meetings | At least 4 a year, with no more than 120 days between two meetings | Most private companies. Small companies have a lighter rule (see below) |
| AGM | First AGM within 9 months of the end of the first financial year. Later AGMs within 6 months of year-end, and no more than 15 months apart | All private companies |
| AOC-4 (financial statements) | Within 30 days of the AGM | All private companies |
| MGT-7 or MGT-7A (annual return) | Within 60 days of the AGM | MGT-7A is for small companies and OPCs |
| Income tax return (ITR-6) | Normally 31 October for companies that need an audit | All companies, even with no income |
| DPT-3 | Normally 30 June | Companies with specified loans or outstanding amounts |
| MSME-1 | Half-yearly, where it applies | Companies with overdue payments to MSME suppliers |

For a company with a 31 March year-end that holds its AGM on 30 September, the normal dates are 30 October for AOC-4 and 29 November for MGT-7.

Some filings are triggered by events instead of the calendar. Examples include appointing or removing a director (DIR-12), allotting new shares (PAS-3) and passing certain resolutions (MGT-14). Check whether a filing is needed before you make these changes.

Private companies that are not small companies should also check whether the Rule 9B demat requirement applies to their shares.

### When is your first AGM?

Your first financial year can be shorter or longer than 12 months, and that changes the date. Here are two examples:

- Company registered on 15 November 2026: the first financial year ends on 31 March 2027, so the first AGM is due by 31 December 2027.
- Company registered on 15 January 2027: because it was registered on or after 1 January, the company can choose 31 March 2028 as its first year-end. The first AGM would then be due by 31 December 2028.


## What Changed for AOC-4 and MGT-7 for FY 2025-26?

MCA General Circular 05/2026, dated 24 October 2026, gives companies more time to file without additional fees:

| Form | Normal date (AGM on 30 September) | File without additional fees until |
| AOC-4 and its variants | 30 October 2026 | 31 December 2026 |
| MGT-7 and MGT-7A | 29 November 2026 | 31 January 2027 |

The relief has three limits you should not miss:

1. It does not extend the AGM deadline. A company that missed its AGM date can still face consequences under the Act.
2. If you file after the relief period, additional fees count from the original due date, not from 31 December or 31 January.
3. The circular does not mention ADT-1 or other ROC forms, so treat their deadlines as unchanged. It also does not cover LLP filings.

If you filed nothing in earlier years, note that the Companies Compliance Facilitation Scheme 2026 had a last date of 15 September 2026 in the latest circular we found. Do not plan around that scheme unless MCA announces a new date.

Late AOC-4 and MGT-7 filings normally attract an additional fee of ₹100 per day for each form. So one company filing both forms late can pay ₹200 per day, on top of any penalty proceedings.


## Is Your Company Now a Small Company?

Check this even if you did not qualify before. From 1 December 2025, MCA raised the limits, so a private company is a small company if its paid-up capital is up to ₹10 crore and its turnover is up to ₹100 crore. Both conditions must be met. The earlier limits were ₹4 crore and ₹40 crore.

Holding companies, subsidiary companies, Section 8 companies and companies governed by a special Act cannot be small companies, even if their numbers fit.

Being a small company lightens some work. The board can meet once in each half of the year, with at least 90 days between the two meetings, and the company files the shorter MGT-7A.

You may also read that the limits will go to ₹20 crore and ₹200 crore. That is a proposal in the Corporate Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026, which was not law in the latest reports we found. Use the current ₹10 crore and ₹100 crore limits until Parliament passes it and it comes into force.


## What Tax Work Runs Alongside MCA Compliance?

MCA filings are only part of the picture. Depending on your business, these dates may come up every month. The dates below apply to the common filing routes.

| Date | Item | Who it applies to |
| 7th of the next month | TDS and TCS payment | Companies deducting or collecting tax |
| 11th of the next month | GSTR-1 | Monthly GST filers |
| 15th of the next month | PF and ESI | Employers covered by those laws |
| 20th of the next month | GSTR-3B | Monthly GST filers |
| 15 December | Third advance tax instalment | Companies liable to pay advance tax |

Income tax dates also moved this year. CBDT Circular 07/2026 extended the tax audit report date to 21 October 2026 and the return date for audit cases to 21 November 2026, for AY 2026-27. Transfer-pricing cases are outside that extension, and their return date is 30 November 2026.


## How Often Do Directors File KYC Now?

DIR-3 KYC is no longer filed every year. Under rules effective from 31 March 2026, a DIN holder files once every 3 financial years, by 30 June after the third year. Personal details such as mobile number, email or address must still be updated within 30 days of a change.

Check each director's next due date on the MCA portal instead of assuming. A late filing or a deactivated DIN carries a ₹5,000 fee, and a deactivated DIN can hold up your company's filings.


## Common Compliance Mistakes New Companies Make

- Treating registration as the finish line and forgetting the 30-day and 180-day items.
- Assuming a company with no business has no filings.
- Reading "no additional fee until 31 December" as an extension of the AGM date.
- Keeping no minutes or registers, then trying to recreate them at year-end.
- Paying company expenses personally and not recording them properly.
- Relying on a due-date calendar without checking the latest MCA and CBDT circulars.
- Letting each director or consultant assume that someone else is tracking the deadlines.


## Frequently Asked Questions

### What compliance is required after private limited company registration?
A new private limited company usually needs a first board meeting and first auditor within 30 days, share certificates within 2 months, and INC-20A within 180 days. Each year it then holds board meetings and an AGM, and files its financial statements, annual return and income tax return.

### Is compliance required if the company has no turnover?
Yes. A company with no turnover generally still needs an audit, an AGM, annual MCA filings and an income tax return. No business activity does not automatically remove these duties. A company that plans to stay inactive should take advice on dormant status or closure instead of simply not filing.

### What is the penalty for not filing INC-20A?
The penalty can be ₹50,000 on the company and ₹1,000 per day on each officer in default, up to ₹1,00,000 per officer. The Registrar can also begin action to remove the company's name from the register if it believes the company is not carrying on business.

### What are the AOC-4 and MGT-7 due dates for FY 2025-26?
The normal dates are 30 days and 60 days after the AGM. Under MCA General Circular 05/2026, companies can file AOC-4 without additional fees until 31 December 2026, and MGT-7 or MGT-7A until 31 January 2027. The AGM deadline itself was not extended.

### How many board meetings does a private limited company need?
Most private companies need at least 4 board meetings a year, with no more than 120 days between two meetings. A small company can hold one meeting in each half of the calendar year, with at least 90 days between them.
