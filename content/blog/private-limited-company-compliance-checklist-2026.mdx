<Lead>
Your Certificate of Incorporation starts the compliance clock. A new private limited company must usually hold its first board meeting and appoint its first auditor within **30 days**, issue share certificates within **2 months**, and file INC-20A within **180 days**. After that, it has board meetings, an AGM, annual MCA filings and an income tax return to manage every year.

This **private limited company compliance checklist** is for founders who have just registered, or who registered earlier and are unsure what they may have missed. It covers the Companies Act deadlines first, then the monthly tax work that usually runs alongside them.
</Lead>

<Callout type="note" title="Key takeaways">

- **First 30 days:** hold the first board meeting and appoint the first auditor.
- **First 2 months:** issue share certificates to the subscribers.
- **First 180 days:** receive the subscription money and file INC-20A, where it applies.
- **Every year:** hold board meetings and an AGM, then file AOC-4, MGT-7 or MGT-7A, and the income tax return.
- **For FY 2025-26:** MCA allows AOC-4 without additional fees until 31 December 2026, and MGT-7 or MGT-7A until 31 January 2027. AGM deadlines have not moved.

</Callout>

### Why INC-20A deserves your attention

INC-20A is the declaration that subscribers have paid for the shares they agreed to take. Under section 10A(2), a missed filing can lead to a penalty of **₹50,000 on the company** and **₹1,000 per day on each officer in default, up to ₹1,00,000 per officer**. Under section 10A(3) and section 248(1)(d), the Registrar can also start action to remove the company's name from the register if no declaration is filed within 180 days.

<Callout type="tip" title="Open the company bank account early">
  That way the subscription money has a clear payment trail before you file INC-20A.
</Callout>

### Do you need ADT-1 for the first auditor?

This is one of the few items where practitioners disagree. The revised Form ADT-1 (substituted via Notification G.S.R. 359(E), effective 14 July 2025) includes a radio button to report a first-auditor appointment. However, Section 139(6) and Rule 4(2) were not amended to expressly mandate it, and the Institute of Company Secretaries of India (ICSI) wrote to MCA on 16 April 2026 seeking clarity. As of the end of October 2026, MCA had not issued a formal clarification.

Our practical view is simple: **file it within 15 days of the board meeting.** The AOC-4 form checks for the auditor's ADT-1 SRN details, so leaving it out can block your first annual filing.

<H2 id="first-board-meeting">What Should Happen at the First Board Meeting?</H2>

Section 173(3) requires at least 7 days' notice for a board meeting, unless the rules for shorter notice are followed. Use the first meeting to settle the items that later filings depend on.

<Callout type="tip" title="Checklist: first board meeting">

- Note the Certificate of Incorporation, MOA and AOA
- Appoint the first auditor and record the appointment
- Take each director's disclosure of interests in Form MBP-1 (Section 184(1))
- Take each director's declaration that they are not disqualified in Form DIR-8 (Section 164(2))
- Approve opening the company bank account and name who can operate it
- Approve the issue of share certificates
- Decide who keeps the statutory registers and minutes
- Agree the financial year and the accounting setup
- Review which registrations apply, such as GST, professional tax or a shop licence

</Callout>

Also put the company's name, registered office address, CIN, telephone number and email ID on your letterheads, invoices, notices and official publications, as section 12(3)(c) requires. Founders often miss this while rushing to design their branding.

For a company with a 31 March year-end that holds its AGM on 30 September, the normal statutory dates are 30 October for AOC-4 and 29 November for MGT-7.

Some filings are triggered by events instead of the calendar. Examples include appointing or removing a director (DIR-12), allotting new shares (PAS-3) and passing special resolutions or specified board resolutions (MGT-14). Check whether a filing is needed before you make these changes.

Private companies that are not small companies should also check whether the Rule 9B dematerialisation (demat) requirement under the Companies (Prospectus and Allotment of Securities) Rules, 2014 applies before issuing or transferring securities.

### When is your first AGM?

Under Section 2(41) of the Companies Act, 2013, your first financial year can be shorter or longer than 12 months depending on whether you were incorporated before or on/after 1 January, and that changes the AGM date. Here are two examples:

- **Company registered on 15 November 2026:** the first financial year ends on 31 March 2027, so the first AGM is due within 9 months of year-end, by 31 December 2027.
- **Company registered on 15 January 2027:** because it was incorporated on or after 1 January, its first financial year automatically runs until 31 March of the following year (31 March 2028). The first AGM is then due by 31 December 2028.

<Callout type="warning" title="The relief has three limits you should not miss">
  **1. The AGM deadline has not moved.** The circular does not extend the statutory AGM deadline under Section 96. A company that missed its AGM date can still face adjudication penalties under the Act.

  **2. Late fees still count from the original date.** If you file after the relief period (after 31 December 2026 for AOC-4 or 31 January 2027 for MGT-7/7A), the ₹100/day additional fee is calculated from the original statutory due date under Sections 137 and 92, not from 1 January or 1 February.

  **3. Other forms are not covered.** The circular does not cover ADT-1, MGT-14, DIR-3 KYC or other ROC forms, so treat their deadlines as unchanged. It also does not cover LLP filings.
</Callout>

If you filed nothing in earlier years, note that the Companies Compliance Facilitation Scheme, 2026 (CCFS-2026) closed on 15 September 2026 (per MCA General Circular No. 04/2026 dated 31 August 2026). Do not plan around that scheme unless MCA announces a new window.

Late AOC-4 and MGT-7 filings attract a statutory additional fee of **₹100 per day for each form, with no upper cap**. So one company filing both forms late pays ₹200 per day, on top of any penalty proceedings against the company and its officers.

<H2 id="small-company-rules">Is Your Company Now a Small Company?</H2>

Check this even if you did not qualify before. Under Notification G.S.R. 880(E), effective 1 December 2025, MCA raised the thresholds under Section 2(85), so a private company is a small company if its paid-up share capital is **up to ₹10 crore** and its turnover is **up to ₹100 crore**. Both conditions must be met. The earlier limits were ₹4 crore and ₹40 crore.

Holding companies, subsidiary companies, Section 8 companies and companies governed by a special Act cannot be small companies, even if their numbers fit.

Being a small company lightens several compliances:

- The board can meet once in each half of the calendar year, with at least 90 days between the two meetings (Section 173(5)).
- The company files the abridged annual return in Form MGT-7A and does not require a cash flow statement in its financial statements.
- Smaller adjudication penalties apply under Section 446B.

<Callout type="note" title="₹20 crore / ₹200 crore is only a proposal">
  You may also read that the limits will double to ₹20 crore paid-up capital and ₹200 crore turnover. That is a proposal in the Corporate Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026 (introduced in the Lok Sabha in March 2026; the Joint Parliamentary Committee submitted its report on 3 August 2026). Because the Bill had not yet been enacted into law as of October 2026, use the current ₹10 crore and ₹100 crore limits until Parliament passes it and MCA notifies its commencement.
</Callout>

Income tax dates also moved for Assessment Year 2026-27 (FY 2025-26). <H2 id="director-kyc">How Often Do Directors File KYC Now?</H2>

DIR-3 KYC is no longer filed every year. Under the Companies (Appointment and Qualification of Directors) Amendment Rules, 2025 (G.S.R. 943(E), effective 31 March 2026), every DIN holder files Form DIR-3 KYC Web **once every 3 consecutive financial years**, on or before 30 June immediately following the third financial year.

Keep two practical rules in mind:

- **Anchored to DIN allotment year:** your 3-year cycle is counted from the financial year your DIN was allotted (for example, directors who completed KYC up to FY 2025-26 are next due between April and 30 June 2028).
- **Mid-cycle changes do not reset the 3-year clock:** any change in a director's personal mobile number, email ID or residential address must still be reported within 30 days of the change, and filing that update does not push back your regular 3-year due date.

Check each director's status on the MCA portal instead of assuming. A missed deadline deactivates the DIN, requires a ₹5,000 late fee to reactivate, and can block your company from filing ROC forms.
